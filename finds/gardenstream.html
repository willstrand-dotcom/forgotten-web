Mike Caulfield contrasts the web’s endless streams with an older, stranger idea: digital gardens where pages are tended, linked, revised, and allowed to become places. It is less a nostalgia trip than a maintenance manual for human knowledge online.

WHY IT STILL MATTERSSearch, social feeds, and AI summaries all reward the newest surface. This essay argues for durable paths, context, and revisitable places — exactly the plumbing a human web archive needs.
Best opened when the internet starts feeling like a river of receipts and you want a path back to somewhere.
